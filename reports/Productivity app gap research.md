# Build the Commitment Ledger Nobody Ships

Build a cross-source **commitment ledger**: a background agent that extracts the promises you made and the asks you received from Slack, calendar/meeting notes and (later) email, keeps each one as a persistent, aging record with evidence links, reconciles the morning plan against what actually happened each night, and nudges follow-ups. Sell it first to **multi-company professionals** (fractional executives, consultants, advisors) at a business-expensed **$30–50/month**, delivered as a thin layer (an MCP server plus a minimal web/mobile ledger view) rather than another calendar or inbox. This is the one gap where the evidence lines up on all four tests: nothing shipping does it end to end (users are hand-rolling it with agent skills, and the only startup attempt, ReadyWhen, has no visible scale); the platforms have commoditized the adjacent features (morning brief, single-shot extraction) but every one of them is designed around once-a-day pushes, few-hour polling and a single tenant, with no persistence of yesterday's commitments; the incumbents' exit pattern (Clockwise, Mayday, Rise) shows scheduling logic is not a moat while accumulated commitment history is; and a 1–4 person team can run the inference for roughly $1–3 per user per month under a $30–50 price. The honest caveat is that this is a bet with three unproven load-bearing assumptions: that extraction precision can be high enough to avoid false-positive fatigue, that the fractional/consultant segment will actually pay (no willingness-to-pay data exists), and that the "ledger with state" is far enough from "brief with summary" that Google, Microsoft and Anthropic do not fold it in within 18 months. Realistic good outcome for a tiny team is **$0.5–1M ARR**; the modal outcome for any solo app is side income, and you should plan for that. Do not build another AI auto-scheduler, a morning-brief app, a generic planner, or an ADHD one-task app as a standalone business; each is ruled out below with reasons.

## The recommendation rests on five assumptions, three of them unproven

The product is a ledger, not a plan. Each row is an obligation (outbound: "I'll send the deck by Thursday"; inbound: "Can you review the contract?") with a source link, counterparty, due date, state (open, aging, done, dropped, renegotiated) and the evidence that closed it (the sent email, the attended meeting, the completed Linear issue). The nightly job does the thing no planner does: it closes rows from observed activity rather than asking you to tick boxes, and surfaces what you owe whom, by when, across every company you work for. Writes back to Slack or email are propose-only; the agent is aggressive about tracking and conservative about acting. Dropping a commitment is a first-class action with no red counter, because the single best-documented emotional failure mode in this category is the "overdue graveyard" that makes people quit ([GitHub kjswalls/dsul #104](https://github.com/kjswalls/dsul/issues/104); [thawly Todoist for ADHD](https://thawly.ai/reviews/todoist-for-adhd)).

The table below states what the recommendation depends on and how well each leg is supported. Treat the "unproven" rows as the first three things to test, not as footnotes.

| Assumption | Status | Evidence basis |
|---|---|---|
| A1. A persistent, cross-source ledger is a different product from a daily brief or one-shot extraction, and platforms will not ship it within ~18 months | Plausible, unproven | Microsoft patented commitment extraction in 2016–17 and ships only prompt-driven summaries; every 2026 platform agent is once-a-day or every-few-hours with no cross-day persistence ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP3295394A1/en); [Anthropic Duet](https://claude.com/blog/claude-duet)) |
| A2. Fractional/consultant professionals will expense $30–50/mo | Unproven | No WTP survey exists; inferred from Motion/Lindy/Fyxer pricing to work budgets and 55x growth in "fractional" LinkedIn profiles ([Digital Journal](https://www.notices.mce.ca.gov/2026/06/the-multi-calendar-professional-is-the-new-normal/)) |
| A3. Extraction precision/recall on real Slack and email is good enough that false positives do not become their own backlog | Unproven, biggest technical risk | No public precision/recall benchmark for task extraction exists; "invent meeting times" is a documented failure mode ([Nylas](https://cli.nylas.com/guides/build-human-in-loop-email-agent)) |
| A4. Inference stays under ~$1–3/user/month with cheap-model routing | Well supported on prices, assumption on volume | Haiku 5.5 at $0.10/$0.50 per MTok makes a daily run ≈ $0.38; the 100K-tokens/day volume figure is the researcher's assumption ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)) |
| A5. Users will grant a personal tool read access across two or more companies' tenants | Partly supported, admin walls real | Slack and M365 both allow admin-gated app approval; Claude's own M365 write tools need Entra admin consent ([Anthropic release notes](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12138966-release-notes)) |

The main tradeoff is sequencing. Gmail is where many commitments live, but reading Gmail is a Google *restricted* scope that requires an annual CASA security assessment with assessor fees of **$500–$4,500** and 2–6 weeks of elapsed time on top of brand verification ([Google restricted scope verification](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/restricted-scope-verification?hl=ko)). Calendar is only a *sensitive* scope and Slack, Linear, Notion and Todoist are rate-limit problems rather than gates. Starting with Slack plus calendar plus task systems gets you to real users months earlier at the cost of missing email-borne commitments; that is the right trade for a solo founder, and the open questions section asks you to verify that the target segment's commitments actually flow through Slack.

## Every platform ships a morning brief; none keeps a ledger

The demand signal is specific and repeated. A 2025 Ask HN thread asked for a *local* tool that processes email and chat across platforms and generates to-dos; a 2026 HN commenter described building a personal "communications analysis" skill that tracks the commitments they make and follows up with people they might miss, which is to say users are hand-rolling the product with agent frameworks rather than buying it ([Hacker News, 2025](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44403821); [Hacker News, 2026](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49589914)). The only open-source precedent, the Slack bot `pledge`, required manual `/pledge` entries rather than extraction ([GitHub buildo/pledge](https://github.com/buildo/pledge)). The lone commercial attempt, ReadyWhen, "watches email, Slack, Notion and meetings for things you said you'd do and drafts follow-ups," and surfaced only through competitor SEO roundups with no funding or user data ([Carly chief-of-staff roundup](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/best-ai-chief-of-staff/); [ReadyWhen blog](https://readywhen.ai/blog/best-ai-chief-of-staff-tools-2026)). The adjacent "plan versus actual" gap is real but under-articulated: no planner in the research reconciles what actually happened against the morning plan, and the circumstantial evidence is users wanting retroactive time logging and running RescueTime alongside their planner because it "doesn't depend on you remembering to start anything" ([Timecap App Store review](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/timecap-time-habit-tracker/id1494584871); [DEV ADHD productivity](https://dev.to/jelloeater/adhd-productivity-and-time-management-43o6)).

Meanwhile the platforms have commoditized everything *around* the ledger. Gemini Daily Brief shipped at I/O in May 2026 to AI Plus, Pro and Ultra subscribers as a morning digest from Gmail, Calendar and Tasks ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/google-gemini-app-daily-brief-redesign-io-2026)). Claude Duet, previewed October 21, 2026, sends "a short brief on the most important items across your connected apps" each morning, checks accounts "every few hours to conserve limits," waits for approval before sending anything, and by Anthropic's own admission "still might misread a thread or miscalibrate what's urgent" ([Anthropic: Introducing Claude Duet](https://claude.com/blog/claude-duet)). Copilot in Outlook's agentic preview flags emails without a reply after 24 hours and drafts follow-ups, yet Microsoft's May 2026 update "confirmed that Copilot still can't create tasks directly in Microsoft To Do" ([Thurrott](https://www.thurrott.com/cloud/335438/copilot-in-outlook-can-now-proactively-manage-your-inbox-and-calendar); [Collab365](https://spaces.collab365.com/posts/microsoft-copilot-automates-email-sorting-and-draf-eql9Zd)). OpenAI shut down Pulse in June 2026 and pointed users at scheduled tasks ([Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260619-chatgpt-scheduled-tasks/)). Notion AI "can read Slack and Jira but cannot take action in them" ([eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/notion-ai-review)). The common shape is a once-a-day push or a prompt-on-demand summary; none of them documents whether yesterday's extracted commitment got done, who is still waiting on you, or how old the ask is.

The user-complaint record explains why this matters more than another scheduling feature. The loudest low-star themes in the category are billing traps, weak mobile apps, sync failure and price, not AI ([Trustpilot usemotion.com](https://trustpilot.com/review/usemotion.com); [G2 Reclaim](https://www.g2.com/products/reclaim-ai/reviews?qs=pros-and-cons)). Where AI is criticised, it is for rigidity and unexplained moves: Motion "packs their day too tightly, leaving no room for the unplanned work that inevitably shows up," and "get the inputs wrong and the AI's choices start to feel random" ([theexpertranking](https://theexpertranking.com/tools/motion/); [catchagent](https://www.catchagent.ai/blog/reviews/motion-app-review)). The research literature says plans break structurally: Gloria Mark's observational work found **57% of working spheres interrupted**, resumption typically only after two or more intervening activities, and screen attention spans down to about **47 seconds** ([CHI 2005](https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Mark-CHI05-p321-mark.pdf); [UC Newsroom](https://www.universityofcalifornia.edu/news/how-sharpen-your-attention-and-meet-your-goals-2024)). A tool whose unit is the commitment rather than the time block addresses the measured problem (what did I promise, what changed, what is still owed) instead of the idealised one (a perfect schedule that survives the day).

## Acquihire-and-kill exits prove scheduling is not a moat; a ledger of obligations is

The question a founder must answer is why Anthropic, Google or Microsoft will not simply add this. The honest answer has two parts: they could, and the structure of their products makes it unlikely to be done well for this buyer.

First, the pattern of what gets absorbed. Single-player AI calendars with 40,000 organisations were worth their teams but not their products: Salesforce hired the Clockwise team into Agentforce and the product shut down **March 27, 2026 with about a week's notice** and all customer data deleted ([The Register](https://theregister.com/2026/03/20/salesforce_clockwise_team); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/salesforce-recuits-team-behind-calendar-app-clockwise-into-agentforce)). Apple bought Mayday Labs in April 2024 and the service ceased within weeks ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2024/4/mayday-calendar-app-announces-acquisition-and-upcoming-termination-of-all-services); [MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2025/05/09/apple-acquired-mayday-labs)). Rise shut down in March 2025 for lack of traction ([AlternativeTo Rise](https://alternativeto.net/software/rise-calendar/news/)). The same happened to thin summary layers: theGist, a cross-tool Slack/email summariser with $7M raised, returned capital in September 2025, with a post-mortem attributing the death to being "commoditized by native features in Slack, Google Workspace and ChatGPT" ([Calcalist](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/jeyngl375); [IdeaProof](https://ideaproof.io/failure/thegist-ai)). Notion killed its own mail client in 2026 because users ran agents on the inbox instead ([Engadget](https://engadget.com/2201940/notion-mail-is-shutting-down)). The lesson is not "avoid this space"; it is that scheduling logic, summaries and new inbox UIs are the layers platforms absorb, while the layers that survived (Cursor's workflow embedding, Obsidian's local-first data, Saner.ai's niche) own structured user data the vendor will not replicate for one segment ([Fueler Obsidian](https://fueler.io/blog/obsidian-usage-revenue-valuation-growth-statistics); [GetLatka Saner.ai](https://getlatka.com/companies/saner.ai)). A commitment ledger accumulates exactly that kind of data: months of who-owes-whom history, counterparties, closure evidence and the user's own corrections.

Second, the structural reasons the platforms are unlikely to serve the multi-company buyer. Each platform agent is bound to one identity in one suite. Google's universal Gemini agent gets "its own Workspace identity" inside a single company's tenant and is sold as a Gemini Enterprise feature ([AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/google-cloud-unveils-universal-gemini-agent-with-its-own-gmail-calendar-and)). Copilot lives inside a Microsoft 365 tenant whose admin must consent; Claude's own M365 write tools need "admin and Entra consent" ([Anthropic release notes](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12138966-release-notes)). A fractional CFO working across three clients' Slack workspaces and two Google tenants is outside every one of those identity models by construction, and vendors describe the result as "each client calendar sits on its own domain and has no context for the others" ([CalendarBridge](https://calendarbridge.com/calendar-syncing-for-fractional-resources/)). The platforms also throttle themselves on purpose. Duet polls every few hours and admits it may "miss an email that arrives between checks"; every platform agent gates writes behind approval cards ([Anthropic Duet](https://claude.com/blog/claude-duet); [Vexa docs](https://docs.vexa.ai/how-to/email-triage.md)). That leaves room for a product that is faster and more precise on *tracking* while matching their caution on *acting*.

The counter-case deserves stating plainly. Microsoft has held the patent on extracting commitments and requests from communications since 2016–17, so the idea is known to the company with the best access to corporate email ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP3295394A1/en)). Anthropic's "Dreaming" memory consolidation and ChatGPT's memory-with-sources mean a third party cannot differentiate on "it remembers you" alone ([Anthropic Code with Claude recap](https://www.claude.com/blog/code-w-claude-sf-2026-sf)). And an HN commenter's objection that Slack and Teams are "not living documents, they are an ephemeral stream of async messages" is a real risk to extraction quality, not just a quibble ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44403821)). The defence is the multi-tenant buyer plus the accumulated ledger, not a technical secret; if you cannot win that buyer within a year, the platforms' GA of Duet, Spark and Copilot agents will close the window.

## Multi-company professionals can pay from a work budget; consumers will not

Every productivity app in this research with revenue above $10M sells to a work budget. Fyxer, an AI executive assistant living inside Gmail and Outlook, went from **$1M to $17M+ ARR** in about eight months and raised a $30M Series B ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/ai-exec-assistant-startup-fyxer-raises-30m-to-expand-to-the-us); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/fyxer-ai-revenue-growth-valuation/)). Motion raised **$60M at a $550M valuation** only after pivoting to SMB "AI Employees" ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250905188051/en/Motion-Raises-$60M-at-$550M-Valuation-to-Build-the-Agentic-Work-Suite-for-Businesses)). Lindy charges $49.99–$199.99 per month with no permanent free tier ([CloudTalk](https://www.cloudtalk.io/blog/lindy-ai-pricing/)). The consumer side is the opposite story: a16z's card-panel data shows only **4.5% of eligible US consumers** paid for any frontier chatbot in August 2026, with a median payer at about $25/month ([The Neuron](https://theneuron.ai/news/ai-top-1-percent-spend-903-a-month-a16z-data/)), Pally made every feature free in October 2026 after launching at $25/month ([Arlo](https://www.arlo.sh/compare/best-ai-assistants-you-can-text)), and the prosumer planner band has settled at **$15–20/month annual** (Sunsama $17, Akiflow $19, Morgen $15) with no headroom above it ([Super Productivity](https://super-productivity.com/blog/best-sunsama-alternatives-2026/)).

The fractional and portfolio-career segment is the strongest "nobody has done it" niche in the notes. LinkedIn profiles mentioning "fractional" leadership rose from roughly **2,000 in 2022 to more than 110,000** two years later, and the cited downside is "coordinating schedules, meetings and overlapping digital calendars across multiple organizations" ([Digital Journal](https://www.notices.mce.ca.gov/2026/06/the-multi-calendar-professional-is-the-new-normal/)). Supply consists of calendar-merge utilities and human fractional executive assistants ([OneCal](https://www.onecal.io/use-cases/fractional-executive); [Boldly](https://boldly.com/blog/fractional-executive-assistants)). These buyers invoice clients, expense tools, and feel the pain of "what did I promise Client A versus Client B" daily. Meeting-heavy managers are the second-ring segment: Asana's 10,624-worker index put **58% of the day** into coordination "work about work," and incumbents capture action items from individual meetings but not the cross-meeting ledger ([BusinessWire Asana](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220405005399/en/5182748/Asana-Anatomy-of-Work-Index-2022-Work-About-Work-Hampering-Organizational-Agility); [tl;dv Copilot review](https://tldv.io/blog/honest-review-of-ms-copilot-meeting-notes/)). Expect the per-tenant data boundary (Client A's Slack never informs a brief sent into Client B's context) to be a selling point rather than a constraint for this buyer.

Pricing should be a flat base that covers normal use plus a credit top-up or bring-your-own-key option for heavy users. Hybrid base-plus-usage showed a **21% median revenue uplift versus 18% flat** across SaaS in Maxio's 2025 data ([Freemius](https://freemius.com/blog/state-of-micro-saas-2025/)), and an Indie Hackers analysis warns that flat pricing "inverts the moment you put an agent behind it," with p99 runs costing $30+ ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/the-uncomfortable-truth-about-ai-tool-pricing-in-2026-92944b6a4d)). Avoid the billing mechanics that generate the category's angriest reviews: trials that auto-convert to annual plans, "monthly" plans billed yearly, and support that cannot refund ([Trustpilot usemotion.com](https://trustpilot.com/review/usemotion.com); [Trustpilot Akiflow](https://ch.trustpilot.com/review/akiflow.com)). RevenueCat's data says 17–32 day trials convert at a **42.5% median versus 25.5%** for trials of four days or less ([RevenueCat 2026 benchmarks](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)), so a two-to-four week no-card trial is both the ethical and the higher-converting choice.

## A tiny team can build it for pennies in inference, but the base rates are brutal

The cost side is favourable. At Anthropic's October 2026 list prices, Claude Haiku 5.5 is **$0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output** for prompts under 100K tokens, with cache hits at 0.025–0.1x and a 50% Batch discount; Sonnet 5.5 is $2/$10 and Opus 5.5 is $4/$20 ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). Under the researchers' assumption of 100K tokens of triage-relevant text per user per day, one consolidated nightly run costs about **$0.38 per user per month on Haiku 5.5**, roughly $7.50 on Sonnet, and about $37 on Fable 5.1; polling every few hours multiplies that 2–3x. Routing the bulk classify-and-extract pass to a small model, reserving a frontier model for ambiguous items and the final brief, and caching the running context keeps inference under **$1–3 per user per month**, a rounding error against a $30–50 price. Deterministic date parsing with the LLM choosing among candidates, rather than generating dates, is the cheapest mitigation for the "invent meeting times" failure and is a credible differentiator because nobody measures wrong-date rates ([Nylas](https://cli.nylas.com/guides/build-human-in-loop-email-agent)). Memory should be a small, user-visible table of typed entries with a nightly dedupe pass, because long-running agent memory is documented to "degrade or die quietly, while everyone keeps trusting it" ([arXiv 2609.05510](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05510)).

Integration walls are documented and manageable in the right order. Slack's official hosted MCP server, Notion's hosted OAuth server and Linear's remote MCP server (free on every plan) are production-grade; there is **no official Google Calendar or Gmail MCP server** for third-party builders, so you run your own Google OAuth app ([ChatForest](https://chatforest.com/categories/communication-collaboration/); [Coworker.ai](https://coworker.ai/blog/linear-mcp)). Gmail push requires renewing the watch at least every seven days ([Gmail push guide](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/guides/push?authuser=2)); Microsoft Graph subscriptions expire and emit `missed` lifecycle events under throttling ([Graph lifecycle events](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/webhooks-lifecycle?tabs=http)). Slack Marketplace review is a moment-in-time check with AI-specific disclosure requirements and a stated 2–3 month typical timeline, though one vendor cleared it in two weeks ([Slack review process](https://slack.com/blog/developers/slack-app-directory-review-process); [Viktor blog](https://viktor.com/blog/slack-marketplace-ai-agent)). Publishing your own MCP server is the cheapest distribution bet available: Anthropic's connector directory lists 950+ servers "used by millions of people every day" and gives publishers a performance dashboard, though there is no public evidence yet of indie revenue flowing through it ([Claude connectors docs](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/overview)).

The revenue comparables are sobering and you should read them before the build. Daily-planner incumbents that have survived seven to ten years sit at roughly **$0.9–2M in estimated revenue with 7–14 people**: Sunsama at $1.1–1.5M, Routine at about $880K, and Saner.ai, the ADHD-niche assistant, at about $880K with eight people ([GetLatka Sunsama](https://getlatka.com/companies/sunsama); [GetLatka Routine](https://getlatka.com/companies/routine.co); [GetLatka Saner.ai](https://getlatka.com/companies/saner.ai)). Those are third-party estimates, not disclosures. Across about 970 Stripe-verified solo products on Indie Hackers, **80.9% made under $500 MRR** and 6.4% cleared $5,000 ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/making-bank-as-a-solo-founder-seems-to-be-rare-3f1ef6ee3e)). RevenueCat's 2026 report across 115,000 apps found the median subscription app earns **$492/month** and 57.7% never reach $1,000 total; AI apps earn 41% more per customer but churn 30% faster, with **21.1% annual retention versus 30.7%** for non-AI apps ([SaaStr](https://saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-how-115000-mobile-apps-deliver-16b-in-revenue-whats-working-whats-quietly-killing-growth); [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/)). At $35/month and 5% monthly churn, reaching $500K ARR means holding about 1,200 paying professionals, which at a 6–8% trial conversion implies 15,000–20,000 trial starts. The documented failure mode is not technical: an analysis of 60 founder post-mortems found products that "work technically but generate no meaningful revenue," with founders retreating into building when acquisition proves hard, and a seven-year bootstrapped founder named delayed customer discovery as mistake number one ([shubhq.com](https://shubhq.com/saas/research/saas-failure-postmortem/); [Glass Box Medicine](https://glassboxmedicine.com/2026/02/21/why-i-shut-down-my-bootstrapped-health-ai-startup-after-7-years-a-founders-postmortem/)).

Distribution that has worked for small teams in this category is slow and compounding: building in public on X plus App Store optimisation took HabitKit to $10–15K MRR over years ([Indie Hackers HabitKit](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-building-in-public-got-my-habit-tracking-mobile-app-to-15k-mo-DCOYyF9O14dBnuGkkaQR)), while Product Hunt reliably delivers a few hundred signups and near-zero paid conversion, one launch producing 400 signups and one paying customer ([Indie Hackers PH](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/400-signups-from-product-hunt-1-paying-customer-what-4-days-taught-me-about-launch-vs-traction-7c0aacf745)). For a fractional-executive buyer the channels are narrower and better: the communities and newsletters where fractional operators find clients, and the MCP directory where the technically fluent among them already live.

## Open questions to answer before a PRD

## Evidence quality: read this before acting on any number above

The researchers could not fetch most primary pages directly. Vendor pricing pages, G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, App Store review pages, Reddit, Hacker News comment threads, TechCrunch, arXiv, RevenueCat, Sacra and Indie Hackers were all blocked by the sandbox proxy, so nearly every claim comes from search-result snippets or from secondary coverage rather than full reads. Consequences: Reddit coverage is thin and second-hand (Motion's r/UseMotion quotes all come via one competitor review blog, usecarly.com); G2 "cons" are G2's AI-generated summaries, not individual reviews; Motion's Trustpilot score varies between 2.1 and 3.9 across snapshots. Price figures conflict for Sunsama, Amie, Vimcal, Tiimo, Reclaim, Shortwave, Any.do and Todoist, often with two or more divergent numbers from competitor blogs, so treat the $15–20 band as a range rather than a quote. Revenue figures for Sunsama, Routine and Saner.ai are GetLatka estimates that contradict themselves across pages (Sunsama is listed at both $1.1M and $1.5M, and as both funded and unfunded). The inference cost model rests on an unsourced assumption of 100K tokens of triage text per user per day; Anthropic's prices are from the official page fetched in full, but OpenAI and Gemini prices are secondary and conflicting. Claude Duet was three days old at research time and has no independent hands-on review. The "80% of AI wrappers die" and "15–25% factual error" figures circulating in the sources could not be traced to a primary document and are deliberately excluded. Nothing here quantifies demand for the commitment ledger directly; the gap is inferred from the absence of supply, hand-rolled solutions, a dormant patent, and vendor-articulated pain, which is the right kind of evidence for choosing what to investigate and the wrong kind for skipping customer discovery.

## Conclusion

The AI wave did not leave the day-planning category empty; it left it full of the same three things (auto-scheduling, morning briefs, extraction on demand) at every price from free to $50, and the market has already shown which of those it will not pay a third party for. What it conspicuously did not produce is state: a durable record of what a person owes and is owed, across the several organisations a growing share of professionals now work inside, closed out by evidence rather than by willpower. That absence is partly a trust and privacy problem the platforms have chosen to solve by polling less and asking permission more, and partly an identity problem their tenant-bound agents cannot solve at all. Both are openings for a small, careful product.

The implication for how you spend the next ninety days is uncomfortable but clear. The scarce input is not code, which the cost and integration evidence says is tractable, but two pieces of knowledge nobody has published: whether multi-company professionals will pay for a ledger, and whether extraction from their real messages is precise enough to be trusted. Spend the first month on interviews and a labelled eval set, and let the PRD fall out of those answers; the base rates say the founder who builds first and discovers later is the one who ends up with a working product and no revenue.
