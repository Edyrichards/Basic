# Business viability of indie / small-team AI productivity apps (2025-2026)

Research date: 2026-10-24. Method note: direct page fetches were blocked in this environment (DNS/proxy refusals on revenuecat.com, sacra.com, indiehackers.com, starterstory.com, growthunhinged.com, saastr.com), so every figure below comes from search-result extracts of the cited pages rather than a full read of the primary document. Where a figure is self-reported by a founder, a third-party estimate (Latka, Sacra, GetLatka, CB Insights), or secondary coverage of a report, it is labelled as such. Treat Latka/GetLatka numbers as estimates, not disclosures.

## Q1. Which indie or small-team productivity / AI-assistant apps publicly report meaningful revenue in 2025-2026?

### Takeaway
Very few of the named daily-planner / notes apps disclose revenue; the ones with public or estimated numbers cluster at roughly $0.9M-$2M/yr with 7-14 person teams (Sunsama, Routine, Saner.ai, Obsidian low-estimate), while the true breakouts (Fyxer, Granola, Obsidian high-estimate) are either VC-funded or ambiguous. Solo mobile indies with verifiable traction sit at $1K-$28K MRR, and the aggregate data says ~80% of Stripe-verified solo products make under $500 MRR.

### Inferences
- A realistic "good outcome" for a 1-4 person AI planner/notes business, based on the apps that do have numbers, is ~$0.5M-$2M ARR with a sub-15-person team; the $10M+ outcomes in this category (Fyxer, Granola, Motion) all took venture capital and attacked the business-email/meeting wedge rather than personal planning.
- Daily-planner incumbents (Sunsama, Akiflow, Morgen, Routine) are 7-10 years old, still sub-$2M (by estimates), and already crowded, so a new generic daily planner is competing for a small pie.
- Solo iOS indies hit five-figure MRR mostly in habit/alarm/utility niches with $1-$3/mo or lifetime pricing and high volume, not in high-ARPU "AI planner" positioning.

## Q2. What do RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2025/2026 (and similar) say about productivity conversion, retention, churn, price points?

### Takeaway
RevenueCat 2026 (115K apps, ~$16B revenue) says the median subscription app earns ~$492/month (down 22% YoY) and 57.7% of new apps never reach $1,000 total; AI apps earn 41% more per customer and convert trials 52% better (8.5% vs 5.6%) but churn 30% faster with 21.1% annual retention vs 30.7% for non-AI. Productivity-specific data is thin: it skews heavily monthly (77%-91% of revenue from monthly plans, sources conflict) and leads reactivations.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 report scale: 115,000 mobile apps, ~$16B revenue — [SaaStr summary](https://saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-how-115000-mobile-apps-deliver-16b-in-revenue-whats-working-whats-quietly-killing-growth); [RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- Median subscription app earns $492/month, down 22% YoY; 57.7% of new apps never reach $1,000 in total revenue (secondary coverage of the 2026 report) — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/); [SaaStr](https://saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-how-115000-mobile-apps-deliver-16b-in-revenue-whats-working-whats-quietly-killing-growth)
- AI apps: 41% more revenue per customer, churn 30% faster (one source scopes the 30% to annual subscriptions); annual retention 21.1% for AI apps vs 30.7% non-AI — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/); [TechNewsWorld](https://www.technewsworld.com/story/ai-apps-generate-revenue-but-struggle-with-retention-180236.html); [xix.ai](https://xix.ai/ainews/revenuecat-2026-report-ai-app-retention-rate-at-21-churn-30-faster-than-traditional-apps.html)
- Trial-to-paid median: 8.5% AI vs 5.6% non-AI — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/)
- Trial length: 17-32 day trials convert at 42.5% median vs 25.5% for <=4-day trials (2026); 2025 edition had 45.7% for 17-32 days; share of apps using <=4-day trials rose from 42.1% (2025) to 46.5% (2026); authors flag correlation not causation — [RevenueCat 2026 benchmarks blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026); [Sub Club podcast](https://subclub.com/episode/the-2026-state-of-subscription-apps-report)
- 80% of trial starts happen on day one (2025 coverage) — [Singular](https://www.singular.net/blog/subscription-apps/)
- Price vs conversion (2025): download-to-trial median 9.8% for high-priced apps vs 4.3% for low-priced; but lower-priced apps retain better — [Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-report-ais-dominance-retention-challenges-and-the-shift-away-from-pure-subscriptions); [Sub Club 2025 episode](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/the-2025-state-of-subscription-apps-report/id1538057974?i=1000699502678)

- Productivity plan mix: 77% of productivity subscriptions/revenue on monthly plans (Utilities 2026 page) vs 91% of revenue from monthly (main 2026 page) — figures conflict — [RevenueCat 2026 Utilities](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-utilities); [RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- 2025 report: Business, Health & Fitness and Productivity "perform well, as users seek long-term solutions"; Photo & Video and Productivity lead monthly reactivations — [RevenueCat 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)
- Trials reduce LTV by 13.7% in Productivity (vs 21.2% Lifestyle) — third-party Medium analysis of RevenueCat/Adapty data, not RevenueCat itself — [Medium / Pavel Chuvak](https://chuvak-pavel.medium.com/state-of-app-monetization-2026-key-trends-from-revenuecat-and-adapty-8bdc23a4bb6f)
- Growth is concentrating at the top of the market (RevenueCat data) — [Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/blog/revenuecat-data-shows-subscription-app-growth-concentrating-at-the-top-news)
- Other retention benchmarks (different definitions, not directly comparable): cross-industry day-30 retention ~5-7% (D1 25-26%, D7 11-13%) — [Phiture](https://phiture.com/mobilegrowthstack/managing-retention-rate-benchmarks-and-expectations/); productivity apps D1 17.1%, D30 4.1% (source unnamed) — [FoxData](https://foxdata.com/en/blogs/why-users-stop-using-productivity-apps-and-how-to-fix-it); productivity tools 4-7% monthly voluntary churn (2026) with first-30-day churn highest — [MWM glossary](https://mwm.ai/glossary/churn); only ~5% of weekly subscribers active after 1 year, 65% churned by day 30; ~43% of monthly subscribers active at day 90 (Adapty, 2025 data) — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/markdown/why-churn-rate-looks-different-what-youre-measuring.md)

### Inferences
- A productivity app priced at $10/mo monthly with the median trial conversion (~6-8%) and ~5% monthly churn implies ~20-month average lifetime and ~$150-$200 LTV per paying user; the heavy monthly skew in productivity means annual-plan prepayment (the usual cash-flow lever) is weaker here than in Health & Fitness.
- The AI-app pattern (higher ARPU, faster churn) suggests pricing AI features to capture early value (longer trials of 2-4 weeks, higher first-month price) while expecting annual retention around one in five.
- The "$492/month median, 57.7% never reach $1K" base rate means a solo builder should plan for the modal outcome being a side-income app, not a salary.

### Gaps
- No productivity-category-specific churn, 12-month retention, or trial-to-paid figure was found in either the 2025 or 2026 RevenueCat report extracts; the full PDF would be needed.
- Appfigures and Sensor Tower productivity-category revenue data did not surface in any result.

## Q3. What price points do consumers vs prosumers vs teams pay for AI planners, and how do inference costs affect margins and pricing models?

### Takeaway
Current list prices cluster at $15-$25/mo annual ($22-$34 monthly) for individual AI planners (Sunsama $17/$22, Akiflow $19/$34, Morgen $15/$30, Amie $20/$25, Motion $19-$29/seat with credit-metered AI), $10-$22/seat for team schedulers (Reclaim), and $1-$3/mo or $30-$50 lifetime for consumer habit/utility apps. Inference cost is the swing factor: a16z's consumer data shows the median AI payer spends ~$25/mo, but power users drive costs (top 10% of users ~50% of provider cost in one simulation), pushing builders toward hybrid base-plus-credits or BYOK.

### Cited Findings
**Price points (list prices as of 2026, individual plans unless noted)**
- Motion: Pro AI $29/seat/mo or $19 annual (pricing page checked Oct 19, 2026) per one source; another says $29/mo annual or $49/mo monthly with AI metered at 7,500 credits/month plus published overage; no free tier — [Arahi comparison](https://arahi.ai/blog/time-blocking-apps-compared); [Reassign comparison](https://reassign.ai/resources/best-ai-scheduling-tools)
- Reclaim (acquired by Dropbox): free Lite (1 user, 1-week range, 1 scheduling link); Starter $10/seat/mo yearly ($12 monthly); Business $15 ($18 monthly); Enterprise $22 yearly — [Arahi](https://arahi.ai/blog/time-blocking-apps-compared); [Efficient.app](https://efficient.app/best/calendar)
- Sunsama $17/mo annual, $22 monthly, 14-day trial no card, no free plan (official manifesto says $25 monthly / $240 yearly — the two differ, check live page) — [Super Productivity blog](https://super-productivity.com/blog/best-sunsama-alternatives-2026/); [Sunsama manifesto](https://help.sunsama.com/docs/pricing-manifesto)

**Consumer willingness to pay for AI**
- a16z Top 100 Gen AI Consumer Apps (Oct 2026, YipitData card panel): 4.5% of eligible US consumers held a paid personal ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude subscription in Aug 2026 (up from 2.1% a year earlier); median payer ~$25/month; top 1% of payers average $903/month and account for 19.5% of consumer AI spend (more than the bottom 50% combined at 16.6%); nearly half of US consumers use AI but only 25% daily; of 44 AI-native products in top web rankings, 84% sell subscriptions, 64% sell usage/credits, 14% ads, 2% transaction fees — [The Neuron](https://theneuron.ai/news/ai-top-1-percent-spend-903-a-month-a16z-data/); [TechNews TW](https://finance.technews.tw/2026/10/18/ai-subscription-models-tested-as-few-consumers-pay/); [Recurly](https://recurly.com/blog/news-blog-how-consumers-are-fueling-ai-revenue/)

**Inference cost and margin**
- Indie Hackers (2026): for human-typed usage API is cheaper than $20 flat subs, but "it inverts the moment you put an agent behind it"; one author's tracking: median cost per run pennies, p95 single-digit dollars, p99 $30+ (anecdotal) — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/the-uncomfortable-truth-about-ai-tool-pricing-in-2026-92944b6a4d)
- Worked example: 5x rise in per-request cost under flat pricing produced ~-18% gross margin — [1752vc](https://www.1752.vc/learn/ai-gross-margins-and-inference-costs)
- Credits simulation (assumed usage distribution): top 10% of users cause 49.8% of provider cost; 11.3% of users lose money under flat pricing — [Masterprompting](https://masterprompting.net/blog/usage-based-pricing-for-ai-apps-credits-model)
- Credits only protect margin if the rate card tracks real cost; retries are the commonly-skipped term that flips a credit negative — [Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/ai-pricing-models); [Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/how-to-calculate-ai-agent-cost-per-task-before-it-wrecks-your-margin/)
- Vendor claim: companies used to 80-90% gross margins now seeing 50-60% or worse; fuzzy outcomes like "improved productivity" are hard to price per outcome and suit usage-based models (sales pitch for outcome pricing) — [Fraction](https://www.hirefraction.com/blog/ai-is-killing-saas-margins-outcome-based-pricing-is-how-you-get-them-back/)
- a16z: "You are not a model. Don't price per token" (argues against raw token pass-through pricing) — [a16z](https://a16z.com/you-are-not-a-model-dont-price-per-token/)
- Maxio 2025 (via Freemius): 24% of SaaS use hybrid base + usage; hybrid showed 21% median revenue uplift vs 18% flat — [Freemius](https://freemius.com/blog/state-of-micro-saas-2025/)

**BYOK (bring your own key)**
- Model: developer charges platform fee, user is billed by the LLM provider; keeps SaaS-style margins; downside: "excludes most everyday users, who may not know what an API key is" — [DEV / c9dn](https://dev.to/c9dn/stop-paying-for-your-users-ai-usage-5bl5); [Kinde BYOK pricing](https://kinde.com/learn/billing/billing-for-ai/byok-pricing/)
- Anecdote: bundled $150/mo plan vs $79/mo platform fee + ~$40/mo user's own OpenAI bill — [GPT-trainer guide](https://guide.gpt-trainer.com/byok-pricing-guide)
- Promotional claim of 97.8% gross margin under BYOK ($0.42 infra per seat vs $19 list) — unverifiable deck — [open-design.ai](https://open-design.ai/plugins/previews/open-design/example-html-ppt-zhangzara-long-table/)
- OpenRouter BYOK fees changed in 2026: Oct 2025 policy was 1M free BYOK requests/month then 5%; retired July 14, 2026; now first $25K/month of BYOK usage free on Standard/Business, 5% above — [OpenRouter blog](https://openrouter.ai/blog/announcements/1-million-free-byok-requests-per-month/); [TrueFoundry (Oct 2026)](https://www.truefoundry.com/blog/openrouter-byok-explained-cheaper-often-faster-and-changing)
- Raycast AI supports "all major LLM provider subscriptions as well as local models" (BYOK-style for prosumers) — [Raycast](https://www.raycast.com/core-features/ai)

### Inferences
- The market has settled on ~$15-$20/mo annual as the prosumer ceiling for a planner; a new entrant has little pricing headroom above Sunsama/Akiflow unless it sells to a business budget (Motion/Fyxer pattern) or to teams per seat.
- For a 1-4 person team, the lowest-risk structure is a flat base price covering light AI use plus a credit top-up or BYOK option for heavy users; pure flat-rate AI with an agentic loop is the model most likely to go margin-negative on the top decile.
- Lifetime pricing ($30-$250) works for local-first/on-device tools (Superwhisper, Obsidian add-ons) precisely because they carry no recurring inference cost; it is dangerous for cloud-inference products.

### Gaps
- No independent dataset on how often BYOK is chosen vs vendor-billed credits, or its effect on conversion.
- No published gross-margin figures for any specific indie AI productivity app.
- No survey measuring consumer willingness to pay specifically for an AI planner (vs general chatbots).

## Q4. What distribution works for this category in 2025-2026?

### Takeaway
Evidence favours build-in-public on X plus App Store ASO (HabitKit, Ellie, Habit Pixel), creator/influencer distribution (Erly, Thomas Frank's YouTube-to-templates funnel), and embedded-in-the-inbox virality for B2B assistants (Fyxer); Product Hunt reliably produces awareness and a few hundred signups but near-zero paid conversion (0.25%-3%). Claude's MCP connector directory (950+ servers, dashboard analytics) is a new, vetted channel but has no public evidence yet of indie revenue flowing through it.

- Product Hunt outcomes: 400+ signups, 1 paying customer in 4 days (~0.25%) — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/400-signups-from-product-hunt-1-paying-customer-what-4-days-taught-me-about-launch-vs-traction-7c0aacf745); 3rd place -> 210 signups, 7 purchases, $543 — [Product Hunt / IndieMerger](https://www.producthunt.com/p/indiemerger/we-placed-3rd-on-product-hunt-and-earned-543); 7th place -> 150 signups — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/7th-place-on-product-hunt-but-still-got-150-signups-from-it-4c02804aba); 1,000+ signups when the product targets founders/indie hackers (PH's own audience) — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/so-i-guess-the-product-hunt-launch-is-still-worth-it-heres-why-and-how-b4757a04ab); launches burn 50-120 hours of prep; "great for brand awareness, don't expect a ton of signups" — [MySignature](https://mysignature.io/blogproduct-hunt-launch/); [Unicorn Platform](https://blog.unicornplatform.com/product-hunt-campaign-insides-traffic-signups-sales/)

- Living inside an existing surface (Gmail, Chrome, Notion, Claude via MCP) lowers acquisition cost because the user never has to adopt a new app; MCP directory listing is low-cost to attempt but unproven as a revenue channel as of Oct 2026.

## Q5. How do apps built on ChatGPT/Claude get commoditized when the platform ships the feature, and which survive?

### Takeaway
The documented pattern is that a model vendor ships the feature natively (ChatGPT, custom GPTs, native memory/notes), the wrapper's paywall becomes optional, and churn follows (Jasper: ~$120M peak 2023 -> $35-55M 2024). Survivors cited are those with proprietary data/feedback loops, deep workflow embedding (Cursor), local-first/privacy positioning (Obsidian, Superwhisper), or a tight niche (Saner.ai for ADHD). The oft-quoted "80% of wrappers die" figure could not be traced to a primary source.

### Cited Findings
- Mechanism: an LLM vendor ships your core feature natively and "your paywall becomes optional overnight because users can get 80% or more of the value free inside ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini"; GPT Store cited as accelerant — [44degrees.ai](https://44degrees.ai/blog/wrapper-apps-one-model-update-away); [TianPan.co (Apr 2026)](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026/04/12/the-ai-wrapper-trap-when-your-moat-is-someone-elses-api-call)
- Jasper: "without a moat beyond prompt engineering and templates, users churned when ChatGPT improved"; revenue fell from ~$120M peak (2023) to ~$35-55M (2024) depending on source — [DEV / dev_tips](https://dev.to/dev_tips/the-graveyard-of-ai-startups-startups-that-forgot-to-build-real-value-5ad9); [Hatchworks](https://hatchworks.com/blog/gen-ai/ai-wrapper-product-strategy/)
- Survivor test: "if OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic all shipped exactly what you're building tomorrow, would your users stay?"; Cursor started as a GPT-4/Claude wrapper and crossed $2B annualized revenue at $29.3B valuation — [Value Add VC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/how-to-build-a-startup-in-a-market-where-ai-will-eventually-do-what-you-do); [Kingy AI](https://kingy.ai/news/research-report-the-ai-wrapper-and-application-market/)
- Data moat example: a legal wrapper built lawyer-correction feedback loops so the AI improves per engagement — [Hatchworks](https://hatchworks.com/blog/gen-ai/ai-wrapper-product-strategy/)
- "Three business models survived" framing (workflow, data, distribution) — [DEV / jamilxt](https://dev.to/jamilxt/ai-wrappers-are-dying-three-business-models-survived-instead-5db4)
- "80% of AI wrappers die" attributed to CB Insights/Gartner but not confirmed in primary reports; survival tables appear to be author estimates — [Value Add VC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/how-to-build-a-startup-in-a-market-where-ai-will-eventually-do-what-you-do); [M Accelerator](https://maccelerator.la/en/blog/startup-strategy/why-ai-wrappers-don-t-have-moats/)
- Local-first survivors: Obsidian bootstrapped, user-supported, low infra cost from local-first design — [Fueler](https://fueler.io/blog/obsidian-usage-revenue-valuation-growth-statistics); Superwhisper differentiates on offline/on-device vs Raycast's cloud-only dictation — [Superwhisper vs Raycast](https://superwhisper.com/vs/raycast)
- Niche survivor: Saner.ai positions as "A Personal AI Assistant for ADHDers" (notes, email, todo, GPT in one place) — [OpenAI community post](https://community.openai.com/t/saner-ai-a-personal-ai-assistant-for-adhders-notes-emails-todo-list-gpt-internet-browsing-in-one-place/673847); Latka ~$880K 2025 revenue — [GetLatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/saner.ai)
- Repositioning as survival move: Amie shifted from calendar/todo to bot-free AI meeting notes and removed its free plan (competitor source) — [nocal](https://nocal.app/alternatives/amie)
- AI tool shutdown trackers exist (AI Graveyard, Killed by AI) listing discontinued tools; Dot (AI companion listed under productivity) shut down Oct 2025 over founder vision divergence, not commoditization — [tooldirectory.ai](https://tooldirectory.ai/ai-graveyard); [killedbyai.net](https://killedbyai.net/)

### Inferences
- For a planner/assistant, the defensible layer is the user's accumulated structured data (tasks, calendar history, preferences) plus integrations the model vendors will not build for one niche; the chat/summarise/draft layer itself has near-zero defensibility.
- Local-first and privacy positioning double as a cost moat (no inference bill on the vendor) and a commoditization moat (platform features are cloud-only).

### Gaps
- No quantified survival rate for AI wrapper apps from a primary source.
- No indie productivity founder essay found that explicitly attributes a shutdown or revenue drop to ChatGPT/Claude shipping an equivalent feature in 2025-2026.

## Q6. Typical failure patterns for indie productivity apps

### Takeaway
The documented failure mode is not technical: products work but never find paying users (one founder: 8,000 visitors, 200 signups, 10 installs, $0 in 12 months), founders retreat into building, and customer discovery happens too late; on the retention side productivity apps show ~17% D1 and ~4% D30 retention (source unnamed), 30% of annual subscribers cancel in month one, and AI features raise churn 30%. Platform API changes are a real but less-documented risk (Spotify Feb 2026 example).

### Cited Findings
- Analysis of 60 Reddit founder post-mortems: common pattern is products that "work technically but generate no meaningful revenue"; one founder shut down after 12 months with 8,000 visitors, 200 signups, 10 installs, $0 revenue; when acquisition proves hard, "founders retreat into technical work that feels concrete and controllable" (self-reported patterns) — [shubhq.com](https://shubhq.com/saas/research/saas-failure-postmortem/)
- Cydoc (health AI, bootstrapped, 7 years) postmortem: first mistake was delayed/minimal customer discovery; talked to real potential customers a year after suspecting who they were — [Glass Box Medicine (Feb 2026)](https://glassboxmedicine.com/2026/02/21/why-i-shut-down-my-bootstrapped-health-ai-startup-after-7-years-a-founders-postmortem/)
- Retention cliff: productivity apps D1 17.1%, D30 4.1% (underlying source unnamed) — [FoxData](https://foxdata.com/en/blogs/why-users-stop-using-productivity-apps-and-how-to-fix-it); new subscribers in first 30 days show highest churn — [MWM](https://mwm.ai/glossary/churn); 30% of annual subscribers cancel within the first month (RevenueCat 2025) — [Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-report-ais-dominance-retention-challenges-and-the-shift-away-from-pure-subscriptions)
- AI-feature churn penalty: 30% faster churn, 21.1% annual retention — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/)
- Plateau from churn: ScreenshotOne reached $32K MRR then plateaued, attributed to 9% churn — [Superframeworks case studies](https://superframeworks.com/case-study)
- Revenue base rate: 57.7% of new apps never reach $1,000 total; median $492/month — [SaaStr](https://saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-how-115000-mobile-apps-deliver-16b-in-revenue-whats-working-whats-quietly-killing-growth)
- Platform risk: solo dev's Discord bot killed by Spotify's Feb 2026 API changes (endpoint removed, 429 bans, extended quota requires registered business and 250K+ MAU) — [Spotify community](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/February-2026-API-Changes-Killed-My-First-Public-Project-Any/td-p/7366944); Apple froze one indie's developer account (he rebuilt a portfolio to $60K/mo) — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/building-an-app-portfolio-to-60k-mo-after-apple-froze-his-developer-account-LD7oNYzKSmWucRfKV1AO)
- Pricing uncertainty persists even at $15K MRR ("I still haven't found the best pricing point"); mid-year MRR flattening — [Röhl](https://sebastianroehl.substack.com/p/2024-my-indie-app-business-year-in)
- Founder-alignment shutdown: Dot wound down Oct 2025 because founders' "shared Northstar had diverged" — [tooldirectory.ai](https://tooldirectory.ai/ai-graveyard)
- Trial misuse: trials reduce LTV by 13.7% in Productivity (third-party analysis) — [Medium / Chuvak](https://chuvak-pavel.medium.com/state-of-app-monetization-2026-key-trends-from-revenuecat-and-adapty-8bdc23a4bb6f)

### Inferences
- The two-week retention cliff is visible in the data (D7 11-13% cross-industry, D30 ~4-7%) and is compounded for AI features; an indie planner must get a user to a recurring habit (daily planning ritual, inbox sync) inside the first week or lose them before the first renewal.
- "Vitamin vs painkiller" is an opinion frame, not a measured one; the measurable proxy is whether users pay before/without a trial and whether monthly churn is below ~5%.
- Platform dependency (Google Calendar, Gmail, Apple Shortcuts) is a tail risk that bites hardest for solo devs who cannot meet "registered business / 250K MAU" style quotas.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 postmortem of a named indie productivity app citing retention as cause of death.
- No documented 2025-2026 case of a productivity app killed by Google Calendar/Gmail API or Apple review changes (searched; only Spotify surfaced).
- No primary source for the "vitamin not painkiller" framing with data.

## Q7. B2B2C or niche-vertical angles with higher willingness to pay
